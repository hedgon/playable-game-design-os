## 4. Existing AI-era claims that are stale or stronger than the evidence

1. `src/31-topics-ai.js` around line 693 (topic `ai-for-implementation`) and `src/51-paths-engineering.js` line 910 (`studio-practice-ai-era`, "This is where AI most reliably saves time, prototypes in hours..."). Stronger than the evidence. Only E4 (a bounded greenfield task, 55.8% faster) and the fact that prototypes are disposable support it. E1 measured the opposite on mature repos (19% slower), E2 is inconclusive, and E9 shows the velocity gain in repos is transient. Fix: say "on greenfield, disposable code the evidence is best; on mature code, measure first". This is my reading of those lines; only the sentence itself was read, not the whole topic.
2. `src/31-topics-ai.js` around line 27 ("Redirect time saved on production into testing...") and lines 96 to 99 assume time is actually saved. The site correctly warns that saved time defaults to scope. Add that whether time is saved at all is unproven for experienced devs (E1, E2).
3. `src/39b-topics-craft.js` lines 3, 13, 139, 266 ("when an agent writes half the code"). "Half the code" is unsourced framing. E13 says 51% of professionals use AI daily, not that half the code is AI-written; E13 also finds 52% avoid agents or use simple assistants only. Reword as "when much of the code may come from an agent".
4. The METR fact on the site is correct and already carries a 2026 update, but it does not give the Feb 2026 numbers (returning devs -18% time, CI -38% to +9%; new devs -4%, CI -15% to +9%) or the reason METR calls it weak evidence (selection). Add them so it does not read as "AI slows seniors down".
5. The DORA facts are accurate (2024: -1.5% throughput, -7.2% stability; 2025: throughput positive, stability negative). Missing: "amplifier" and the capabilities framing, which is the actionable part. Also DORA is correlational; the site should say so.
6. SWE-bench: the site cites Verified as a "human-validated" 500-task subset (OpenAI Dec 2024 system card). If E16 is confirmed, that is now out of date: OpenAI reports the benchmark is contaminated and flawed and stopped reporting it. Re-fetch the primary page and update `models-reasoning-and-scaling` before publishing anything new that quotes SWE-bench Verified scores.
7. METR time horizon: the site correctly hedges (16 h limit). Keep; do not turn into a claim about "agents working for a day" (METR states it measures task difficulty on well-specified tasks).
8. `craft-engineer-in-the-ai-era` interview answer cites the 2025 controlled study as "developers using early-2025 tools were slower... while believing they were faster". Accurate. Add that a 2026 update is weaker and points the other way, to avoid overcorrecting.
9. GDC 2026: site cites layoffs (28% laid off in two years). It does not cite the AI sentiment numbers (52% negative, programmers 59%). A senior working in a studio needs that context: it is a team-morale and disclosure factor (fits `ai-disclosure-policy`).

## 5. Proposed topics and deepenings (six at most)

New topics:

1. `craft-measuring-ai-uplift` (domain craft). Teaches: how to test whether AI helps your team on your code: paired tasks, holdout, quality and stability metrics, the traps (lines added +281% then gone, PR counts, self-reports). Why: the studies disagree (E1 vs E3 vs E4) so local measurement is the only defensible answer; the site cites the studies but has no method. Evidence: E1, E2, E3, E4, E9, E11, E12.
2. `craft-security-review-of-generated-code` (craft). Teaches: reviewing AI code for security, with game-shaped targets: save-file parsing, mod and UGC loaders, server authority and client trust, network input, secrets in engine config, dependency suggestions. Include: the model's confidence is not a signal. Why: missing; the site covers prompt injection but not insecure output. Evidence: E6, E7, E8 (vendor), OWASP Top 10 named in E8.
3. `lead-junior-skill-formation-with-ai` (leadership). Teaches: designing a junior's work so they still learn: explain-first use of the assistant, generation-then-comprehension, no-AI reps, review by cold explanation, a growth plan. Why: E5 is the only RCT on this and the site has nothing; `lead-onboarding` and `lead-conflict-growth` assume no AI. Evidence: E5 (single small RCT, so label as early), E3 (juniors gain most).
4. `craft-ai-cost-latency-budget` (craft). Teaches: one budget for an AI feature or AI-assisted pipeline: tokens per request, cache hit rate, per-player and per-day caps, p95 latency, batch vs live, routing across models, and a re-check date. Why: parts are spread across `models-prompt-caching`, `models-security-and-operations` and `ai-budgets-and-debugging`; no single build. Evidence: vendor pricing pages (label vendor), the tokenizer change already cited on the site; capability drift from E15.
5. `craft-benchmarks-and-vendor-claims` (models or craft). Teaches: reading a benchmark: contamination, flawed tests, scaffold dependence, 16-hour ceiling, weak-evidence disclosure. Why: senior devs choose tools; E16 (once verified), E15, E2. May instead be folded into `models-reasoning-and-scaling` as a deepening; prefer that if E16 cannot be confirmed.

Deepenings (no new topic):

6. `craft-verification-as-the-job` and `craft-what-matters-now`: add the quality-drift evidence (E9, E10, E12 stability) and the "safety nets" point. Also `ai-for-implementation`: fix the overstated line (section 4, item 1). `lead-code-review`: one AI-era step (large AI diffs, review by cold explanation, check changed tests and thresholds). `lead-incidents`: one AI-era step. `models-reasoning-and-scaling`: the SWE-bench update and the METR Feb 2026 numbers.

Not proposed as new: architecture decision records under a frame budget (compose from `lead-conventions` and `craft-performance`), spec-writing (composed from `craft-engineer-in-the-ai-era`, `ai-agentic-implementation`, `ai-evals`), game constraints (already covered).

## 6. Path outline

id: `senior-game-developer-ai-era`. title: "Senior game developer in the AI era". pick: "Own the architecture, checks and team when AI writes code" (about 58 characters, please count before shipping). track `engineering`, level `advanced`, hours about 22.
Audience: engineers with a shipped title who have led features and now own architecture, verification and other engineers' growth while agents produce much of the code.
Outcome: You can specify work for an agent and a human, build the checks that prove it, review AI changes for correctness, security and performance, budget an AI feature, decide from measurements where AI helps your team, and grow juniors who still learn.
prereq: none (advanced). prereqAny: `gameplay-engineer-godot`, `gameplay-engineer-unity`, `live-game-backend-engineer`, `netcode-server-engineer`, `build-and-release-engineer`, `game-ai-programmer` (each existing prerequisite path must then list this path in `next` if the validator requires it, as with the designer path). next: `technical-lead`, `studio-practice-ai-era`, `interview-prep-engineer`. Hours reflect hours per stage below.

Stages (each with 3+ steps, checkpoint, build):

1. Architecture and boundaries (4 h). Goal: keep a codebase reviewable when changes come fast.
   Steps: `craft-engineer-in-the-ai-era`, `craft-design-patterns`, `craft-entities-and-scenes`, `craft-performance` (frame budget half), `lead-conventions` (decision records), a reflect on the module map.
   Build: an architecture decision record for one system (for example a save or entity system) written against a frame budget and a memory budget, with the rejected option and the measured cost, plus the agent boundary (files it may touch).
2. Specify and verify (5 h). Goal: turn a feature into a spec an agent or a colleague can build, and prove it.
   Steps: `ai-agentic-implementation`, `craft-verification-as-the-job`, `craft-tools-that-work-with-ai`, `ai-evals`, checklist `agent-rules-file`, tool `verify`, `craft-teaching-agents`, `craft-game-loop-timestep` (determinism/replay as a check).
   Build: an agent-ready spec plus an eval suite for one game feature: failing tests first, a property check with a replayable seed, and a golden set if the feature uses a model; then run it against an agent and record what it got wrong.
3. Review and security (4 h). Goal: review AI-written changes for correctness, security and performance.
   Steps: `lead-code-review`, `craft-what-matters-now`, new `craft-security-review-of-generated-code`, `models-security-and-operations`, `craft-over-defensive-code`, `craft-save-systems`, checklist `ai-verify`.
   Build: a written review of an AI-written change (yours or a public repository's) with a security checklist (untrusted input, save/mod loading, server trust) and a performance check on a target device capture; state what you would block.
4. Measure, budget and choose (4 h). Goal: decide from measurements where AI helps, and what an AI feature costs.
   Steps: new `craft-measuring-ai-uplift`, `models-reasoning-and-scaling`, `models-prompt-caching`, new `craft-ai-cost-latency-budget`, `models-open-and-closed`, checklist `ai-architecture-boundary`, `ai-budgets-and-debugging`.
   Build: (a) a paired-task plan to measure AI uplift for your team, with metrics that include review time and stability; (b) a per-player cost and p95 latency budget for one AI-assisted feature, with caps and a re-check date.
5. Grow the team (3 h). Goal: keep juniors learning while the team ships faster.
   Steps: new `lead-junior-skill-formation-with-ai`, `lead-onboarding`, `lead-one-on-ones`, `lead-conflict-growth`, `craft-clean-code-ai-era`.
   Build: a mentoring plan for one junior: explain-first assistant use, no-assistant reps, a review by cold explanation, a milestone showing skill (not output).
6. Own incidents and technical direction (3 h). Goal: run the bad day and argue trade-offs with design and production.
   Steps: `lead-incidents`, `pm-postmortems`, `quality-and-build-health`, `certification-and-review`, `lead-saying-no`, `pm-cross-discipline`, `scope-control` (one game read by lens if it really shows the point, for example a shipped simulation with a documented technical trade-off; check the lens exists before adding a game step).
   Build: a one-page technical direction note for a design request that is expensive to build, with the cost, options, the cut, and the rollback plan if an AI-assisted change breaks a live build.

Total 23 h across stages (4 + 5 + 4 + 4 + 3 + 3 = 23 h); the outline says about 22 h; set to 23 unless a stage is trimmed. Step minutes must total within 10% of each stage's hours (validator rule stated in the sibling designer research). Games: only add where a lens really shows the point; none required.
Solo variants: every build should state a solo version (own project, an open-source repo, or a recorded agent session).
