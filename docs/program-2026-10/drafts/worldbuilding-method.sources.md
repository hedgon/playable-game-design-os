# worldbuilding-method: sources

"Read" = fetched and read the page. "Summary" = only a search-result or fetch summary was seen; recheck.

1. Wolf, Building Imaginary Worlds (Routledge, 2012): invention, completeness, consistency as the three working tests; completeness defined as the degree to which the world contains explanations and details covering aspects of its characters' experiences; consistency as plausibility and absence of contradiction; a world of little detail can be consistent but thin, and heaps of detail can still be dull. https://www.routledge.com/Building-Imaginary-Worlds-The-Theory-and-History-of-Subcreation/Wolf/p/book/9780415631204 (publisher page; fetched table of contents did not match the claims) and a search summary of the book text. SUMMARY ONLY. Fact-checker: confirm the wording against the book.
2. Wolf's infrastructures: maps, timelines, genealogies, then nature, culture, language, mythology, philosophy (the latter five "backgrounded" and cumulatively creating difference). Source: search summary of the book's text (scribd/pdfcoffee copies and a Journal of Tolkien Research review, https://scholar.valpo.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1070&context=journaloftolkienresearch, which returned 403). SUMMARY ONLY. Chapter number not confirmed (a fetch listed it in a "World Structures and Systems of Relationships" chapter; not stated in the draft).
3. Wolf, "illusion of completeness", ellipsis and gaps: the draft says reviewers summarise completeness as often an illusion built from selected details and gaps. Source: search summary; the Harvard interview page (https://tagteam.harvard.edu/hub_feeds/2020/feed_items/264615/content) was unreachable. SUMMARY ONLY.
4. Jenkins, "Game Design as Narrative Architecture" (2004): designers as narrative architects; evoked, enacted, embedded, emergent spatial stories; examples American McGee's Alice (evoked), Myst and Half-Life (embedded), The Sims (emergent). https://web.mit.edu/~21fms/People/henry3/games&narrative.html (read via fetch summary of the essay itself). Year 2004 from the First Person volume (not re-checked).
5. Koster on the Ultima Online ecology: AI disabled because of the cost of radial searches followed by pathfinding; removed in beta after being rewritten by an engineer who did not like or understand it; later failure was player hoarding (wool and shirts) depleting the supply that spawns sheep. https://www.raphkoster.com/?p=46439 (read via fetch summary; first-hand). The "rabbits and wolves" popular version is from search summaries of raphkoster.com/?p=517 and ?p=519, unread in full. The draft calls the popular story contested; it does not assert Koster's account of the hoarding beyond the summary.
6. Story bible advice: two to three paragraph summary first; tentpoles, key objects, major events, locations; living document across writing, design, art, sound, marketing. Bryant Francis interviewing Anna Megill, Ubisoft Massive lead writer, Game Developer, 18 Oct 2019. https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/building-a-basic-story-bible-for-your-game (read via fetch summary).
7. Outer Wilds talks, GDC 2020: Alex Beachum, "The 4D Level Design of Outer Wilds" (pacing and delivery of narrative information in a physics simulation that changes the world over time); Beachum and Loan Verneau, "Curiosity-Driven Exploration: The Design of Outer Wilds" (early 2D prototypes to the final mystery structure). https://gdconf.com/news/see-4d-level-design-outer-wilds-deconstructed-gdc-2020 and https://www.gdconf.com/news/attend-gdc-and-learn-how-outer-wilds-nailed-curiosity-driven-game-design (first returned 200 summary; second returned 404 on fetch, the description came from search results). The talks themselves were not watched. SUMMARY ONLY.
8. Mobius Digital on designing curiosity (clues that point without naming, visible landmarks): https://www.mobiusdigitalgames.com/blog/the-intentionality-of-wandering (read via fetch summary). Not quoted in the draft; supports the "predict, then find" framing.
9. Hollow Knight, Game Informer, 16 Oct 2018, "The Making of Hollow Knight": map interconnection; map as the biggest design challenge, solved with a cartographer's map bought and filled by exploring; Forgotten Crossroads becoming Infected Crossroads; Boneforest cut and Deepnest reduced. https://gameinformer.com/2018/10/16/the-making-of-hollow-knight (read via fetch summary). The exact phrase "the single biggest design challenge" came from the summary; recheck wording. A second URL, /2018/10/15/, exists for the same piece.
10. Carson, "Environmental Storytelling: Creating Immersive 3D Worlds Using Lessons Learned from the Theme Park Industry", Gamasutra, 1 March 2000. https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/environmental-storytelling-creating-immersive-3d-worlds-using-lessons-learned-from-the-theme-park-industry. Read via summary; not cited in the draft's facts (environmental-storytelling covers it).
11. FromSoftware / Miyazaki: lore carried in item descriptions and fragments; Miyazaki says he was inspired by reading books he only half understood and filling the gaps. Source: search summaries only (denofgeek.com/?p=781317 and others). The draft says only "FromSoftware's reputation" for fragmentary lore and does not attribute a quote. SUMMARY ONLY; find a first-hand interview if the coordinator wants this stated as fact.
12. CrossCode area design: the brief names CrossCode's areas. The itch.io interview returned 403 and search found nothing on region design, so the draft does not make claims about CrossCode. Not verified.
13. Draft numbers that are advice, not sourced facts: "three to five world rules", "a one-page canon", "two deliveries per fact", "half the testers" reach threshold. Rules of thumb, flagged as such in the prose.
