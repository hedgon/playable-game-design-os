Key: READ = I fetched and read the page itself (through the fetch tool, which returns a model summary of the page, not raw text). SUMMARY = I only saw a search-result summary or a secondary page. Fact-checker: prioritise SUMMARY lines and the UNVERIFIED lines.

## Magic: The Gathering

- Power creep causes named by Wizards (four-copy rule and near-copies, fixing underperformers, missed interactions; "power creep is relative" across formats; creating rotating Standard was "by far, the most important thing"; Alpha had no creature they would not reprint but overpowered spells such as Black Lotus, Ancestral Recall, Time Walk) — author Sam Stoddard, "Dealing With Power Creep", 2013-08-09. READ. https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/feature/dealing-power-creep-2013-08-09
- Play Design set a target power level, deliberately powered up sets from Guilds of Ravnica through Throne of Eldraine, admitted missing on Oko ("much stronger than we intended"), and named Field of the Dead and Veil of Summer as problems — "Play Design Lessons Learned", 2019-11-18. READ. https://magic.wizards.com/en/articles/archive/feature/play-design-lessons-learned-2019-11-18
- Standard rotation moves from two to three years; no rotation at the release of Wilds of Eldraine; four named sets rotate in 2024; rationale "more longevity", archetypes built up over time; applies to tabletop and Arena Standard, not Alchemy — "Revitalizing Standard" (May 2023). READ. https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/announcements/revitalizing-standard
- The "Escher Stairwell" technique name and the claim that Rosewater believes power creep is mostly in players' minds: SUMMARY only, from a search-result paragraph. NOT used in the draft as a claim.
- Search result noting that extended rotation means more sets in Standard and so more pressure for strong cards: SUMMARY (fan press, mtgrocks / wargamer). Used only as a contested point in `TECH` cost text ("as Magic fans argued after 2023"). https://www.wargamer.com/magic-the-gathering/standard-plan-failed

## Revision after the fact-check (2026-10-05)

Corrections applied to the draft; these lines supersede the matching lines above.

- Hearthstone: Standard was announced 2 February 2016 and the first rotation came with the spring (April) 2016 expansion, about three months later, with the list of leaving sets. READ (https://hearthstone.blizzard.com/en-us/news/19995505, per the fact-check). The draft no longer says the rule was published "long before".
- Hearthstone 2021: the Core Set (235 cards, yearly refresh) replaced Basic and Classic in Standard; Blizzard retired the Hall of Fame and moved its cards to Wild (Legacy set). READ (https://hearthstone.blizzard.com/en-us/news/23620129/introducing-the-core-set-and-classic-format). Hall of Fame as a list that moved over-represented or design-limiting cards: https://hearthstone.blizzard.com/en-gb/news/23014810/developer-insights-class-identity-hall-of-fame-and-new-cards (READ).
- WoW 6.0.2 (14 October 2014) patch notes: the 450,000 / 3,000,000 to 30,000 / 200,000 Fireball example is Blizzard's own and is a 1-to-15 scale (about 6.7%); the notes give the reasoning that numbers were no longer easy to grasp after four expansions. Source: the full notes reproduced at https://worldofwarcraft.judgehype.com/patch-wow-ptr-602/ (per the fact-checker's read; I did not read it myself).
- The 4% overall and 8% player-health figures are a Blizzard designer's tweet (Celestalon) quoted by the community wiki https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Stat_squish (SUMMARY). The draft attributes them as such and no longer links them to the Fireball example.
- 32-bit overflow (Ra-den near 1.5 billion health against 2,147,483,647): community wiki only. Blizzard's stated reasons are readability and granularity (6.0.2 and Midnight notes). The draft says overflow is a risk to track, not the proven cause.
- Later squishes: 8.0.1 live 17 July 2018 (https://blizzardwatch.com/2018/07/12/officially-less-week-mage-tower-appearances-patch-8-0-launches-july-17/); 9.0.1 live 13 October 2020, levels 120 to 50, cap 60 (https://us.battle.net/support/en/article/275327); Midnight pre-expansion update live 20 January 2026 (https://news.blizzard.com/en-us/article/24244455/midnight-pre-expansion-content-update-notes, READ). Dates from the fact-check.
- Pokémon: regulation sets in Scarlet and Violet are lettered (A to I), not numbered; the handbook says sets "may last between one and three months" and "may be announced up to 30 days prior" (fact-checker read the PDF). https://www.pokemon.com/static-assets/content-assets/cms2/pdf/play-pokemon/rules/play-pokemon-vgc-tournament-handbook-en.pdf
- Honkai: Star Rail: the quoted statement is that difficulty deploying older characters and strengthening them are "already on the schedule"; the "new combat modes" and "power creep would not leave them behind" wording is removed. https://www.gosugamers.net/honkai-star-rail/news/74256-honkai-star-rail-announce-buffs-to-older-characters-are-already-in-the-works (SUMMARY).
- Wizards' power target: qualitative. Play Design says Standard strength is too nebulous to define rigidly, powered up marquee sets from Guilds of Ravnica through Throne of Eldraine (about one year of sets) and missed on Oko. READ (https://magic.wizards.com/en/articles/archive/feature/play-design-lessons-learned-2019-11-18). The numeric index and ceiling are this topic's own proposal and labelled so.
- Oblivion: the remaster changed attribute gain at level-up (12 points per level, secondary: Steam discussions, press), not enemy scaling. "Commonly criticised" has no primary source. UNVERIFIED.
- Elden Ring fixed area strength with upgrade materials as the gate: my knowledge of the game, no source. UNVERIFIED.
- Overwatch as horizontal design: my judgement, stated in the draft as judgement-only.
- Street Fighter and EA Sports FC (Ultimate Team promo cards as sold creep): library examples named in the draft from general knowledge, no source read. UNVERIFIED. Mark Rosewater on power level: no column used, because only search summaries were available; Sam Stoddard's Wizards article and the Play Design article are the Wizards sources.
- Destiny sunsetting: "stated benefit" (Bungie's reasons: testing, file size, encouraging new gear) is from search-result summaries, SUMMARY; removal announced February 2021 by Joe Blackburn in State of Destiny (per the fact-check, Windows Central read raw).
